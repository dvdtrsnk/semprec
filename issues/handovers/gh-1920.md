## What was delivered

- `PATCH /api/proposals/:id/values` accepts, beside `set`, `relations` (ref -> relation property key -> the full list of target item ids, UUID-checked, at most 200 per list) and `steps` (ref -> `{ enabled }`). At least one of the three is required; a body with none keeps the gh-1892 error `details.field` `set`.
- Both are stored in `userOverrides` under the reserved keys `relations` and `enabled` of the ref, merged per relation key and dropped when equal to the assistant's envelope (relation lists compared as id sets). They are composed into the effective envelope (single-step relations, step relations, update steps' `after.relations` and their own relations where the key exists, step `enabled`) and validated through `assertValidEnvelopeForCard`, so a missing target or a relation the target database does not have raises the error confirm raises, and nothing is written.
- `userEditedKeys` carries `ref.relations.<propertyKey>` and `ref.enabled`; the fields and lists of a switched-off step are not counted. The primary step cannot be switched off (found by role `destinationRef`, so also a multi-step primary whose ref is not `primary`); `set` refuses the reserved keys; the history entry lists the touched keys.
- New ADR `docs/adr/2026-11-13-user-overrides-carry-relation-lists-and-step-switches-under-reserved-keys.md`.

## Entry points and contracts dependents build on

- `userOverrides` shape: `{ [ref]: { <fieldKey>: value, relations?: { <propertyKey>: string[] }, enabled?: boolean } }`; `relations` and `enabled` are reserved names (ADR). `mergeUserOverrides(aiEnvelope, existing, edits)` keeps its signature, its third argument is now the request's edits (`set` plus the reserved keys); `computeUserEditedKeys` and `composeEffectiveEnvelope` in `inbox/proposalOverrides.ts` read them.

## Deviations from the specification and spec seams

1. `updateProposalValuesWithClient` is in `inbox/proposalValueUpdates.ts`, not `proposalActions.ts` (gh-1892 handover).
2. Unknown refs cannot be known while the body is parsed, so the handler checks shapes, UUID ids and the per-list cap; unknown refs and the primary step switched off are refused by `updateProposalValuesWithClient` before any write.
3. `set` refuses the keys `relations` and `enabled` as reserved, so a database property named `enabled` (the Sleep settings database has one) cannot be edited through `set`. A stored `userOverrides` whose ref holds a non-boolean `enabled` or a malformed `relations` is now "Malformed userOverrides" (plain Error).
4. A switched-off step's relation lists are left out of `userEditedKeys` as well as its fields.
5. `reviseProposalWithClient` (gh-1924) keeps only property keys of the new destination, so it drops relation lists and step switches and reports them as dropped; changing that is out of scope.
6. An update step's own relations are touched only for a key it already holds; its new targets are written to `after.relations`, which validation requires to agree.
7. `mergeUserOverrides` keeps its signature (the third argument is now the request's edits).
8. Cross-chain seam found at landing (not changed, not tested): `retainOverridesForThreadRevision` (gh-1938) reads the destination ref's whole override object, which now includes the reserved keys `relations` and `enabled`, and hands them to `mergeUserOverrides` as edits. No test covers a thread revision of a card that holds a relation-list override or a step switch, so its behaviour there is unverified. See follow-ups.

## Follow-ups from review

- Medium (fixed): a relation key named like an `Object` member (`constructor`, `toString`, `hasOwnProperty`) crashed the merge with a TypeError; `sameTargets` now looks the list up by own key. `__proto__` is dropped by the zod record parse of the stored envelope for the edit and for confirm alike, so both ignore it; refusing it would be a new validation rule the Task excludes.
- Low (deferred, recorded in the ADR consequences): reserved keys `relations`/`enabled` reinterpret or reject `userOverrides` rows that the gh-1892 route could already have stored for a database with a property of that name. Follow-up: design an escape for property values named `relations` or `enabled` and a forward-only migration of rows stored by the gh-1892 route.
- Follow-up found at landing: add a test for an item-thread revision (`reviseProposalFromThreadWithClient`, gh-1938) of a card whose `userOverrides` hold `relations` or `enabled`, and decide whether those survive it and whether `droppedKeys` names them.
