## What was delivered

- `GET /api/journal/periods/day/:period` now answers `birthdays` next to `events`. In `journal/journalRouteHandlers.ts`, `readJournalDayEvents` became `readJournalDayTimeline`: it makes the one existing `readCalendarTimeline` call for the range `day..day` and splits its items by kind into `events` (kind `event`, unchanged, same twelve fields) and `birthdays` (kind `birthday`; fields `id`, `personId`, `personName`, `title`, `date`, `calendarId`).
- `birthdays` is `[]` for a day without a birthday. The `week`, `month`, `quarter` and `year` responses have no such key. A public holiday and every other kind of timeline item is in neither array. A trashed person does not appear (the timeline's own rule). A person born on 29 February is listed on 28 February of a non-leap year.
- No second calendar read and no new query in the Journal: the birthday rule and the Birthdays calendar id come from the timeline item (gh-1795, gh-1800).

## Contracts dependents build on

- Response shape of the day period: `{ ...period, events: JournalDayEvent[], birthdays: JournalDayBirthday[] }`, where `JournalDayBirthday = Pick<CalendarBirthdayTimelineItem, "id" | "personId" | "personName" | "title" | "date" | "calendarId">`. `id` is the timeline id `birthday:<personId>:<year>`; `calendarId` is the Birthdays calendar id and is `null` when that calendar is trashed or missing.
