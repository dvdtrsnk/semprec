## What was delivered

- Events gain the system-owned, locked date property `nextOccurrenceDate` (seed in `seed/seedCalendars.ts` and `seed/seedTenDatabases.ts`, cs and en labels, cutover for existing tenants). Sorting the Events collection by it lists a series once, at its next day.
- `calendar/nextOccurrenceDate.ts`: `computeNextOccurrenceDate` (a stored series takes its value from `expandEventOccurrences`, so a trashed occurrence item is skipped and a moved one is named on its new day), `computeNextOccurrenceDates` (batch), and `computeNextOccurrenceDateFromRule` (the rule matching that a create and the read-only proposal preparation keep, because they have no occurrence items or no client). The value is the first day on or after today in the tenant zone, else the last occurrence, else the Event's own date; a non-recurring Event has its own date.
- `calendar/nextOccurrenceDateTransform.ts`: a pre-write transform registered under the events module (after the time and iCal identity transforms) that computes the value on a create and when `date`, `endDate` or the recurrence changes in an update, with a preparation for proposals. A client write of the property is refused with 403 `owner_violation` before the transform runs.
- `calendar/nextOccurrenceSeriesHooks.ts`: Events update, delete and restore hooks (registered in `domainWriteHooks.ts`) that recompute the series in the same transaction when an occurrence item is trashed, restored, or has its `date` or `endDate` changed, and when a series is restored from the Trash; a series in the Trash is left alone and nothing is written when the value is unchanged.
- `calendar/nextOccurrenceRefreshAction.ts`: the daily heartbeat action `calendar.refreshNextOccurrence` (validated `action_config`, batches of 200 locked rows paged by id, writes through `updateItemWithClient` as `SYSTEM_WRITER` with `allowedSystemKeys`, one expansion per batch). Exported `NEXT_OCCURRENCE_REFRESH_ACTION_ID`, `NEXT_OCCURRENCE_REFRESH_HEARTBEAT`, `createNextOccurrenceRefreshAction`; listed in `manifest/knownActionIds.ts`, in the calendar manifest `heartbeatActions`, seeded as a daily heartbeat just after midnight in `seedCalendarsInTransaction`, and registered in `semprec-api/src/actionRegistryComposition.ts`.
- `calendar/nextOccurrenceDateCutoverMigration.ts`: inserts the definition by direct SQL, backfills every Event in batches of 500 without moving `updated_at`, creates the heartbeat; idempotent, skipped for a tenant without Events; registered in `db/tenantCutoverMigrations.ts` after the occurrence step of gh-1843 and before the journal cleanup step of gh-1883.
- ADR `docs/adr/2026-11-14-the-next-occurrence-date-of-an-event-is-a-stored-system-property.md` (decision, the three writers, the rule-only create and preparation, and an honest lock limit).

## Follow-ups from review

Two reviewers (requirements, rules). Findings of the requirements reviewer: the value ignored cancelled and moved occurrences, so Task step 2 was not met (medium; fixed in `a5c3250ba`); no test told a stale-only refresh from one that also rewrites an Event whose value is today (medium; fixed in `9260f1f3d`, the mutation `<` to `<=` fails two tests); an ADR sentence on a cancelled occurrence was untrue of the code (low; the ADR was edited in place). The rules reviewer returned `clear`. The focused recheck returned `pass`, with one new low finding: **purging a trashed occurrence item does not recompute its series' value** (the series hooks cover update, delete and restore, not the permanent purge; the timeline shows the day again as a virtual occurrence while the stored value keeps skipping it until the daily refresh moves it on; it needs a cancelled occurrence in the Trash whose day is still ahead when the retention purges it). A purge hook (`registerItemPurgeHook`) would close it; recorded as a follow-up, not fixed. Also recorded: the trash or restore of a calendar locks its Events in id order and can deadlock with a concurrent write of one of its occurrences (PostgreSQL rolls one back; named in the ADR); a legacy series whose stored schedule is invalid falls back to its last occurrence or its own date and the refresh would rewrite it daily (the validation hook blocks such a schedule on new writes). gh-1901 is not landed; its entries go before this source's entries as stated above.
