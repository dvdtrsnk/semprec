## Open for the owner

**The second acceptance criterion is not met as written, and no decision record covers it** (`issues/decisions/gh-2030.md` does not exist). The criterion reads: "When a Gmail or Outlook mailbox is removed, then its watch or subscription is cancelled and its token is revoked at the provider."

1. **Outlook: the token is not revoked. BLOCKED, not delivered.** The implementation makes no revocation call for a `graph_api` mailbox, a test asserts that none is made, and ADR point 6 gives the reason: the Microsoft identity platform has no call that revokes one application's refresh token. The Graph subscription is deleted and the stored credential is deleted by the removal. The worker reported the clause BLOCKED and asked for a controller decision. The requirements reviewer raised no finding, because nothing in the candidate should change, and wrote that amending criterion 2 and Task steps 2 and 4 to a Google-only revocation is the owner's decision.
2. **Gmail: the watch is stopped and the token revoked only for a mailbox that is the only one recording its Google account.** While another `gmail_api` mailbox of any tenant records the account (`accountShared`), or when the removed mailbox's row records no account address (`accountUnknown`), no call is made at Google. This is the fix of the security finding below, in the form the finding proposed; treating `accountUnknown` like shared goes one step beyond it. The fixer and the recheck both wrote that the narrowing needs the owner's acceptance. ADR point 7 records it.

The recheck passed the chain with these two points stated "for the controller, not defects of the delta". The queue runner landed the chain on that verdict and did not decide either point.
