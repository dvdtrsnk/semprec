## Context

Model tier rationale: Changes how a mail sync job claims its pass lease against a row another pass keeps locked for a whole transaction, and which jobs may record a mailbox failure.

Found in the local review of PR #2089. `claimMailSyncPass` (`mail/mailAccountSyncStateStore.ts`) reads the sync state row `FOR UPDATE` with no bound on the wait. A Gmail or Graph pass renews its lease (`renewMailSyncPass` inside `enterSyncPass`, `mail/mailSyncJob.ts`) and records its activity on that row inside its one long reconcile transaction, which also talks to the provider. Every trigger during that pass (push, sweep, write-back) blocks in the claim until the pool's 60 s statement timeout. The resulting `57014` is not `MailboxSyncBusyError`, so the catch block records it with `recordSyncError`, sets `syncStatus` to `error`, raises the Semp problem and writes a `mail_sync_error` notification, although the running pass holds the lease and decides the account's state.

Decisions:

- The claim bounds its wait for the row lock with a transaction-local `lock_timeout` of `MAILBOX_SYNC_PASS_WAIT_MS` (5 s), restored after the grant, as the mailbox sync guard does. On expiry it throws `MailboxSyncBusyError`; the job is skipped like one that finds the mailbox sync guard held (no failure, status, problem or notification; the sweep re-enqueues an account still due). Short holders (an IMAP batch, a release, the Mail switch) are still waited for, so their trigger still reaches the rerun flag.
- Only a job that holds the lease records a failure. A job that fails before it holds the lease logs the failure (identifiers, failure kind, error name) and rethrows for graphile-worker's retry, writing nothing on the account.
- Renewing the lease in its own short transaction was rejected: the Gmail and Graph reconcilers write the same row (`recordGmailActivity`, `recordGraphActivity`) inside the long transaction, so the row stays locked anyway.

## Scope

### In scope

- The claim's wait and the failure recording of a job without the lease.

### Out of scope

- Moving the Gmail and Graph provider calls out of their transaction.
- Recording a trigger that gave up as a rerun request.

## Acceptance criteria

- While another transaction holds the sync state row as a Gmail or Graph pass does, a trigger returns after about 5 s (well below the statement timeout), opens no provider client and leaves the account's last error, failure kind and `syncStatus` unchanged.
- A job whose claim fails with another error (a statement timeout) rethrows it and records no error, failure kind or `syncStatus`, even once the row is free again.
- The existing single-flight, stall-check, mail sync and mailbox service tests stay green.
