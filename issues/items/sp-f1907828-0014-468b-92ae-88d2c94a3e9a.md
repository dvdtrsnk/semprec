## Context

Model tier rationale: Public webhooks routed to tenants require SECURITY DEFINER router functions, renewals and a privilege test.

Google and Microsoft can notify about calendar changes; CalDAV servers cannot for third parties.

## Task

Load `state-writes`, `db-migrations`, `error-handling`, `io-hardening`, `canonical-keys` and `testing`.

1. Google: `events.watch` per calendar with a channel id and token, stored in `calendar_remote_state` (`channel_id`, `channel_expires_at`); public `POST /api/calendar/google/webhook` validates the token and enqueues `calendarAccountSync`; channels renew a day before expiry; stopped on calendar removal.
2. Microsoft: Graph subscriptions on `/me/events` per account with clientState (excluded from export), public `POST /api/calendar/graph/webhook` with validation handshake; renew before expiry.
3. Router functions `route_google_calendar_channel(channel_id)` and `route_graph_calendar_subscription(id)` (SECURITY DEFINER, `semprec_router`), with their migration, a row in `docs/operations/database-roles.md` and a privilege test.
4. CalDAV accounts: the sweep runs them every 5 minutes; the app's foreground trigger (`POST /api/accounts/:id/calendars/sync`) runs them on demand.
5. Webhooks enqueue only; they never fetch.

## Scope

### In scope

- Channels, subscriptions, router functions, renewals.

### Out of scope

- Client realtime (mobile issue).

## Acceptance criteria

- A Google webhook with a valid channel token queues exactly one sync for that account; an unknown channel answers 404 and queues nothing.
- Channels and subscriptions are renewed before expiry and stopped on removal.
- The router functions return ids only and are callable only by `semprec_router` (privilege test).
