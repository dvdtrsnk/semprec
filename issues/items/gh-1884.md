**Model tier:** medium

## Context

Model tier rationale: One read-side extension of the calendar timeline that combines fields from existing issues into a defined response shape, without writes, locking or new persistence.

A task that was not done on its day is carried forward (the nightly move is #1674). The calendar must show where it has been: for each past day from the first planned day to the day before the live day, a trace marks "not done, moved on", "not done, done on 16 October", "not done, moved to event X" or "not done" when the chain ended unresolved; the first day is at its original time, the following days are all-day, and the live task carries where it came from. Today none of it exists; the timeline endpoint is built by #1717, the first planned day by #1602 (`since`, `sinceTime`), the completion day by #1544 (`completedOn`), the effective day and host events by #1780, and the task-in-event state by #1880. Occurrence expansion for recurring tasks (#1886) builds on this shape.

Decisions: the field names are the ones the Tasks schema uses (`since`, `sinceTime`); no other chain-start keys are introduced. The trace chain runs from `since` until the day before the live day, or until `completedOn` if the task is done, otherwise until today. Labels ("moved from Tue 13 October 10:00") are composed by the client, the backend returns only data. A recurring occurrence is carried at most until the day before its next occurrence (rule from #1670's date functions). A task completed before its planned day is shown as done on its completion day (`completedOn`) and not on its planned day; its day chain ends on the completion day and no trace is returned for it (decided by the product owner; the display day comes from `taskDisplayDay` of #1544).

## Task

Load `canonical-keys`, `testing` and `io-hardening`.

1. Extend `GET /api/calendar/timeline` (from #1717) so that a task with `since` set returns, in the requested range, one trace entry per day from `since` up to the day before the live day: `{kind: "trace", taskId, day, firstDay, sinceTime, outcome}` where `firstDay` is `since`, `sinceTime` is `{from, to}` or null (set for the first day only), and `outcome` is one of `carried` (the task moved on to the next day), `doneOn` with `{date}` from `completedOn`, `movedToEvent` with `{id, title}` of the host event, `missed` (the status is `missed`).
2. The live task entry carries `carriedFrom` = `since` (a date) whenever `since` is set and the task is not done on a day earlier than today.
3. Days that fall outside the requested range are omitted but `firstDay` is still returned in full. Traces are produced from stored fields only, no writes, and the query stays bounded by the range cap of the endpoint.
4. Add the response types to the shared contract used by the timeline.
5. Tests: a task `since` 13 October with `sinceTime` 10:00 to 11:00, not done: timeline 13 to 16 October returns a first-day trace at 10:00 to 11:00, all-day traces on 14 and 15 October and the live task on 16 October with `carriedFrom` 2026-10-13; after completion on 16 October the traces have outcome `doneOn` 2026-10-16; moving the task to 20 October at 09:00 keeps `since` and the traces for 13 to 15 October; a task dated 20 October completed on 16 October returns no trace, is returned as done on 16 October and is absent from 20 October.

## Acceptance criteria

- With `since` 2026-10-13 and `sinceTime` 10:00 to 11:00, the timeline for 13 to 16 October returns traces for 13, 14 and 15 October (firstDay 2026-10-13, the 13th with sinceTime) and a live task on 16 October with `carriedFrom` 2026-10-13.
- After the task is completed on 16 October the traces report outcome `doneOn` with date 2026-10-16.
- Moving the task to 20 October 9:00 does not change `since` and the 13 to 15 October traces remain.
- A task dated 20 October and completed on 16 October is returned as done on 16 October, not on 20 October, with no traces.
