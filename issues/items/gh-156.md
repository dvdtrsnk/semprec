**Blocked by:** #241, #155, #82, #83

## Context

Issue #241 exposed item create, detail, and versioned patch. The trash semantics — cascade delete, symmetric restore, and purge — live in this issue as one whole, because they must operate on exactly the same subtree. #157 (queries, `inTrash` opt-in) and #158 build on the completed lifecycle.

## Task

Implement the trash half of the item lifecycle as thin mappings onto choke-point services plus one queue task:

- Soft `DELETE /api/items/:id`: sets `deleted_at` and cascades to any inline databases under the page and their rows, in one transaction. Returns the full authoritative row (never 204).
- `POST /api/items/:id/restore`: restores the whole subtree, symmetric to the delete cascade.
- The 30-day trash purge: a `graphile-worker` task registered via `registerTask` on the queue engine from #21 (`packages/queue`) that permanently deletes items whose `deleted_at` is older than 30 days, including their cascade subtree.
- Every mutation rejects archived databases according to #83 and preserves owner/lock rules, enforced by the choke-point.

## Scope

### In scope

- Delete, restore, purge, and their cascade/concurrency semantics.

### Out of scope

- Create, detail with path, and patch — delivered by #241.
- Queries (including `inTrash: true`), relations, files, a hard-delete endpoint, and generic batch writes.

## Acceptance criteria

- Delete, restore, and purge operate on exactly the same subtree: deleting a page with inline databases trashes all their rows in one transaction, restore brings back exactly that set, and purge removes exactly that set.
- Purge permanently deletes only items with `deleted_at` older than 30 days; younger trashed items and live items are untouched.
- Delete and restore return the full authoritative row; writes into archived databases are rejected.
