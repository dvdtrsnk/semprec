## Context

Model tier rationale: It changes which rows a destructive, tenant-scoped permanent purge removes and which rows it locks, inside a transaction that runs consumer hooks under the tenant's purge lock. The change is one statement and one condition, but a wrong predicate would remove a row that is not due or keep a lock that blocks writers, so the tests must pin both with a real cutoff and real concurrent transactions.

`purgeExpiredTrashSubtree` (`chokePoint/itemTrash.ts`) walks a trashed subtree with unlocked reads, keeping only rows whose `deletedAt` is older than the `cutoff` it was given. In its delete loop it then reads each row of the walk again with `itemsStore.lockItemById` (`SELECT ... FOR UPDATE`) and passes the locked row to `itemsStore.hardDeleteItem` (`DELETE ... WHERE deleted_at IS NOT NULL`), to `deleteItemDependents` and to the item purge hooks (`runItemPurgeHooks`, accepted ADR `docs/adr/2026-11-10-item-purge-domain-hooks.md`). The locked read was added by source sp-962cf217-c004-462b-941b-702f5d7536b8 so that a hook receives the row exactly as it was removed. Its handover (`issues/handovers/sp-962cf217-c004-462b-941b-702f5d7536b8.md`, "Follow-ups" 1 and 2, verified against the code at the integration tip) recorded two defects that were left open, because the review confirmed them and the second predates the locked read:

1. The locked read keeps the row lock of a row a restore made live again between the walk and the row's turn in the loop. The purge does not remove that row (`hardDeleteItem` finds it live), but the lock is held until the purge transaction ends. A write to that row waits for the rest of the call, and a writer that holds another row of the same walk while it waits can deadlock with the purge, which aborts one of them (the sweep retries a failed call on its next run). The ADR names the lock in its Consequences ("the purge does not remove that row and holds its lock until the purge transaction ends").
2. Neither the walk's check nor the delete compares the row's current `deletedAt` with the cutoff at the time of the delete. A row that was restored and trashed again after the walk read it is removed although its trash is fresh (`hardDeleteItem` guards only `deleted_at IS NOT NULL`). The ADR says so ("A row that was restored and trashed again after the walk is still removed, and `item.deletedAt` is then that later instant"). The existing test of that case (`gives the hook the row as it was removed when a restore, an edit and a new trash of it committed after the purge read it` in `__tests__/itemPurgeHooks.test.ts`) uses a cutoff in the year 2999 and so pins nothing about a real cutoff.

Both are one change: decide, in the locking statement itself, whether the row is still a trashed row past the cutoff. A statement `SELECT ... FROM items WHERE database_id = $1 AND id = $2 AND deleted_at IS NOT NULL AND deleted_at < $3 FOR UPDATE` locks only a row that qualifies. The purge transaction runs at READ COMMITTED and each statement takes a new snapshot, so a restore, or a fresh trash, that committed before the statement means the row does not qualify and is not locked; a row that changes while the statement waits for the lock is rechecked against its latest version. The mail purge hook (#1691) lengthens the per-row work of the purge, which is why this belongs before more consumers are added.

The purge's lock order does not change: the tenant's purge lock, then an item row, then that item's dependents, as in `docs/adr/2026-11-01-trash-purge-coordinates-roots-and-transactions.md`. What changes is which rows are locked and which are removed.

## Task

Load `state-writes`, `testing` and `adr-conventions`.

1. In `chokePoint/itemsStore.ts` add `lockTrashedItemBefore(client, databaseId, itemId, cutoff)`: the row-locking read `SELECT <the columns of lockItemById> FROM items WHERE database_id = $1 AND id = $2 AND deleted_at IS NOT NULL AND deleted_at < $3 FOR UPDATE`, returning the mapped row or `null` when the row is gone, live, or trashed at or after `cutoff`. `lockItemById` and its other callers are not changed.
2. In `purgeExpiredTrashSubtree` (`chokePoint/itemTrash.ts`), replace the `lockItemById` call of the delete loop with `lockTrashedItemBefore(client, row.databaseId, row.id, cutoff)`. `null` skips the row with no write, no dependent delete and no hook, as a missing row does today. `hardDeleteItem` and the rest of the loop stay as they are. Update the comments of the loop and the part of the docstring of `purgeExpiredTrashSubtree` that says a race-restored row "stays locked until this transaction ends" and that the locked read "does not decide the removal".
3. Add `docs/adr/<date>-the-permanent-purge-locks-only-a-row-that-is-still-trashed-past-the-cutoff.md` (the date is the day it is written; `area: [backend]`, `supersedes: []`, standalone). Its Context references `[[2026-11-10-item-purge-domain-hooks]]` and `[[2026-11-01-trash-purge-coordinates-roots-and-transactions]]` and says it narrows three statements of the first without editing it: the locked read also locks a row a restore made live and holds that lock to the end of the purge; "the locked read does not decide whether a row is removed"; and a row restored and trashed again after the walk "is still removed". The Decision states the locking read with its predicate and that a row that does not qualify is skipped without a lock, a delete or a hook. Its Consequences state: a hook's `item.deletedAt` is always older than the call's cutoff; a row restored or trashed again after the walk, with a trash newer than the cutoff, is left for a later sweep; the predicate is evaluated by the locking statement, so a restore that commits while that statement waits is rechecked and the row is skipped, but a lock is still taken on the row's newest version in that one case (a restore that commits during the statement's own wait for the lock), which the purge then holds to its end; and the lock order is unchanged.
4. Tests, in `__tests__/itemPurgeHooks.test.ts` (the existing harness; the existing tests keep passing, including the one with the cutoff in the year 2999):
   - a unit-level test of `lockTrashedItemBefore` against a live row, a row trashed before the cutoff, a row trashed after it, a row trashed exactly at it, and a missing row: only the second returns a row and only it is locked (`FOR UPDATE NOWAIT` from another connection fails for it and succeeds for the others);
   - with a real cutoff taken after the page was trashed: during the root's hook call a restore of a nested row commits on another connection; the purge reaches that row, skips it, and a probe `SELECT ... FOR UPDATE NOWAIT` of that row on another connection, run from the hook call of a later row of the same walk, succeeds, so the restored row is not locked; the restored row and its dependents are intact and no hook was called for it;
   - with the same cutoff: during the root's hook call a nested row is restored and trashed again (so its `deletedAt` is newer than the cutoff); the purge does not remove it, no hook is called for it, its dependents are intact, and the rows of its cascade that the re-trash made fresh stay too; a second purge call with a later cutoff removes it;
   - the existing test with the cutoff in the year 2999 (restore, edit and a new trash committed after the walk) still removes the row and gives the hook the row as it was, because its new `deletedAt` is older than that cutoff;
   - a writer that updates the restored row while the purge is still running does not wait for the purge once the row was skipped (the update commits before the purge ends).

## Scope

### In scope

- Locking and removing, in the permanent purge's delete loop, only a row that is still trashed past the cutoff when the locking statement runs.

### Out of scope

- The walk's own check, the tenant purge lock, the order of the purge's locks and the purge of an archived database (`hardDeleteItemsOfDatabase`).
- Any change to `hardDeleteItem`, to the hook registry or to the hook context.
- The logging of a failed purge call in `trash/purgeExpiredTrash.ts` (a separate follow-up of the same handover, not about which rows the purge removes).
- The tenant erase.
- A consumer hook, including the mail purge hook (#1691).

## Acceptance criteria

- When a row of the walk was restored before the purge reaches it, then the purge skips it, calls no hook for it, deletes none of its dependents and holds no lock on it afterwards.
- When a row of the walk was restored and trashed again after the walk, with a trash newer than the cutoff, then the purge does not remove it, calls no hook for it and leaves its dependents; a later sweep with a later cutoff removes it.
- When a row is trashed before the cutoff and nothing changed since the walk, then it is removed with its dependents and its hook receives the row exactly as it was removed, as before.
- When a hook is called, then `item.deletedAt` is older than the cutoff of the call.
- The ADR of this source exists and the accepted ADRs it names are not edited.
